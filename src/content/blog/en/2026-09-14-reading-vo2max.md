VO2max is the maximum amount of oxygen your body can use per minute during intense effort, measured in ml/kg/min. It's one of the most important indicators of cardiovascular fitness for runners.

## How does your watch estimate it?

Watches like Garmin, Coros, or Apple Watch don't measure oxygen intake directly (that requires lab equipment) — they **estimate** it from:

- Heart rate at different paces
- Pace and elevation (via GPS + altimeter)
- Heart rate variability (HRV) on newer models

Because it's an estimate, accuracy improves with consistent outdoor runs and a chest strap heart rate monitor, rather than the wrist-based optical sensor alone.

## Why does the number sometimes jump around?

Hot weather, hilly routes, fatigue, or illness during a run can throw off that session's estimate and cause your overall VO2max to shift suddenly. Don't worry too much about a drop after one hard run — the trend over several weeks is what actually matters.

## Takeaways

- Track the monthly trend, not single sessions.
- Run a few steady, easy sessions to give your watch cleaner data.
- Combine the number with how you actually feel (RPE) instead of trusting it blindly.
